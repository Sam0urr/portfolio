*Draft — work in progress (24 October 2025). Full paper available on request.*

Lobbying regulation sits at the point where interest representation meets state authority. In a functioning democracy, transparency in lobbying is a fundamental principle: it makes influence over policy visible, prevents policy capture, and shores up public trust in the decision-making process. Lobbying itself is legitimate — but if left unchecked, organised self-interest damages trust and feeds the perception that governance is rigged in favour of elite groups.

Brexit created a natural experiment in what happens when supranational oversight is removed. The UK's withdrawal from the EU set off a process of *de-Europeanisation* that eliminated the ETR-shaped constraints on UK lobbying and localised corporate lobbying activity within Westminster. Where the EU has moved toward de facto mandatory transparency through the [European Transparency Register](https://transparency-register.europa.eu/index_en), the UK's [Transparency of Lobbying Act 2014](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2014/4) covers only consultant lobbyists — a fraction of total lobbying activity — leaving significant transparency deficiencies.

**Research question.** *How has the UK's withdrawal from the EU affected the transparency of lobbying practices compared to EU member states?*

**Contribution.** The literature covers the *Europeanisation* of transparency in depth but has largely overlooked what happens on the way out. Existing behavioural work documents where UK lobbying resources shifted after Brexit ([Coen & Katsaitis, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-923X.13132)), and separate institutional critiques diagnose the design weaknesses of the UK regime ([Crepaz & Worthy, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1093/pa/gsad024)) — but the two haven't been connected. This paper links them: what happens when a post-Brexit lobbying wave hits an already-fragmented and weakly-enforced UK regulatory system, and what that says more generally about the resilience of governance standards when supranational political constraints are lifted.
